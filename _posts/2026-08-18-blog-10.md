## Key Points

- The article describes building a DIY 'slit-scan' camera system that captures images by recording vertical lines as the camera moves, similar to how traditional film scanning backs work
- The project was inspired by digital scanning backs from the 1990s and the idea of moving the camera instead of having the subject move
- Uses a Basler ruL2048-19gm industrial line camera capable of capturing 1x2048 pixel lines at 19,000 frames per second
- The system includes accelerometer/gyro sensors to measure speed and movement for proper image reconstruction, though GPS proved unreliable on trains
- Post-processing was a major challenge - needed to integrate accelerometer data, handle parallax effects, and manually select 'distance per pixel' values for different image segments
- Added color capability with a Basler ruL2098-10gc camera but encountered issues with infrared sensitivity requiring UV/IR cut filters
- Color images showed RGB fringes due to the sensor design, requiring manual channel alignment during post-processing
- The camera has been used on various train systems (MBTA Orange Line, Mattapan Line, REM in Montreal) despite looking suspicious
- Current system requires a laptop but future work aims to make it standalone and improve post-processing tools
- Code for both capture and processing (called 'grindstone') is available on GitHub
