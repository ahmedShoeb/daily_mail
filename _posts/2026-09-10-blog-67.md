## Key Points

- Author describes shifting from AI optimism to deep concern about AI's future impact on society
- Previously believed automation had historically improved living standards and thought similar would happen with AI
- Now worries about political consequences of AGI where humans become economically useless without power
- Initially viewed AI alignment as a solvable engineering problem but now sees it as increasingly intractable
- Believes AI capabilities are growing far faster than our ability to control or understand them
- Fears humans will willingly hand over control to AIs due to competitive pressures and AI superiority
- Observes AI writing proliferation as evidence of disempowerment, where even thinking gets outsourced
- Notes software engineering discourse has become shallower with reliance on AI coding assistants
- Expresses concern that people increasingly defer to AI for major life decisions like consulting an oracle
- Contrasts initial excitement about AI ending 'the great stagnation' with current fears about human disempowerment
