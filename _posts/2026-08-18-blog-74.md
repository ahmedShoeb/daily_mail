## Key Points

- The author's Framework Laptop 13 with AMD Ryzen 7040 series failed during a BIOS 3.20 update recommended by Framework, displaying a corrupt screen and becoming bricked
- Framework support refused warranty repair as the laptop was out of warranty, suggesting buying a new motherboard for $500+ instead
- Research revealed multiple users had similar BIOS flashing issues on Framework forums, with no official acknowledgment from Framework
- The author chose to repair the laptop themselves using $20 worth of tools: a CH347 flash programmer, 1.8V level shifter, and WSON8 pogo pin probe
- The BIOS chip is a Winbond 25R256JWEQ 1.8V SPI flash chip in a difficult-to-access WSON form factor requiring pogo pins
- The author had to extract the BIOS image themselves using a third-party tool since Framework didn't provide documentation or raw BIOS files
- During the repair process, Framework experienced a data breach where customer names, email addresses, phone numbers, and addresses were compromised
- The repair required careful orientation and force to maintain pogo pin contact while flashing took less than a minute with the CH347 programmer
- After successful flashing, the laptop booted but BIOS settings, serial numbers, UUID, and bootloader configuration were lost
- The author criticizes Framework for lacking BIOS recovery features, poor repair documentation, and design choices that hinder repairability despite marketing as repairable
- Framework was compared unfavorably to non-repairable competitors like Dell and HP that offer BIOS recovery mechanisms
- The article concludes with calls for Framework to fix their BIOS updater, provide better repair support, and improve their data security practices
