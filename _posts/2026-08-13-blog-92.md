## Key Points

- Nvidia is reportedly testing versions of its upcoming Rubin Ultra accelerator with less memory due to HBM supply constraints
- Some configurations being tested include as little as 192 GB or 256 GB of memory instead of the originally announced 1 TB
- The company is reportedly considering stepping back from HBM4E to HBM4 memory technology
- HBM4E's complexity and supply chain issues are reportedly causing strains on memory manufacturers
- Nvidia confirmed the roadmap for Rubin Ultra but didn't address specific details about memory configurations or potential delays
- Industry reports suggest memory shortages could persist through 2030, with 2027 being the "worst year" according to SK Hynix's CEO
- Nvidia has secured long-term memory supply agreements, including a $500 billion strategic partnership with SK hynix
- Some Nvidia customers reportedly aren't concerned about per-GPU memory reductions, valuing the long-term relationship more
- Rubin Ultra is part of Nvidia's Kyber NVL144 design originally shown with four compute chiplets and 1 TB of HBM4E memory
- There were earlier reports suggesting Nvidia canceled its quad-die Rubin Ultra design due to manufacturing complexities
