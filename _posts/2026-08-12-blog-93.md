## Key Points

- Two AI chip companies, Taalas and Groq, are tackling AI inference bottlenecks with opposite approaches: TaLAS etches model weights permanently into silicon, while Groq keeps weights loadable in SRAM
- Both companies eliminate HBM (high-bandwidth memory) by keeping weights on-chip, addressing the 'memory wall' where GPU inference is limited by memory bandwidth, not compute
- Taalas' approach freezes model weights in mask-ROM fabric, offering extreme speed (16,960 tokens/sec vs ~230 on H200) and low cost ($0.0075 per million tokens) but requires new silicon for model updates
- Groq's LPUs keep weights in loadable SRAM, allowing any model to run with minutes of recompilation but requiring many chips to hold large models (576 chips for 70B model)
- Both strategies were endorsed by major players: NVIDIA licensed Groq's tech for $20B in Dec 2024, AMD acquired Taalas in July 2025
- A 'dark horse' alternative is diffusion language models that generate blocks of tokens at once, potentially making sequential token generation (the problem both chips solve) less critical
- The author argues that while general-purpose LLMs may continue evolving too quickly for silicon etching, narrow functions like speech recognition, translation, and OCR are stable enough to benefit from permanent silicon implementation
- The future may involve hybrid approaches: stable model components etched in silicon combined with loadable, updatable sections
- Small dedicated models (not conversational LLMs) are where the author believes silicon etching will find its strongest economic case
