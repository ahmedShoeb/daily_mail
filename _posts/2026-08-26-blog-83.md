## Key Points

- Inherent, a London AI lab founded by Google DeepMind alumni, claims its AI agent 'Faraday' outperformed larger models from Anthropic and OpenAI in reproducing scientific papers
- The company recently emerged from stealth with a $50 million seed round and operates with just 12 employees in London
- Faraday runs on a comparatively tiny Qwen 3.6 model with 27 billion parameters, outperforming much larger frontier-scale systems
- Beyond accuracy, Inherent focused on teaching Faraday 'research taste' - the instinct for what experiments are worth running and how to design them well
- The company uses reinforcement learning rather than rule-based training, betting this approach will better generalize to its goal of creating AI scientists
- Inherent aims to build AI that can discover new scientific knowledge, not just verify old results
- The company deliberately didn't build its own coding tools, instead using OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Codex, similar to how human scientists leverage existing software
- Founder Edward Hughes advocates ending 'garden leave' restrictions common in the UK that hinder talent mobility compared to the US
- Inherent plans to grow from 12 to 20-25 employees by year-end, positioning itself as a potential landing spot for unsettled DeepMind staff
