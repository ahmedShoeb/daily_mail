## Key Points

- Bill Gates has shifted from being a staunch AI optimist to deeply pessimistic about AI's impact on society
- Gates warns AI could be 'the worst source of injustice' or 'the greatest equalizer ever invented' and says the transition will be 'one of the most turbulent times in human history'
- He predicts widespread, permanent unemployment affecting both white- and blue-collar jobs, calling for policies like taxing AI tokens and robots and reserving specific jobs for humans
- Gates proposes creating an international organization to manage AI risks that cross borders, modeled after agreements for nuclear, aviation, and ozone protection
- He acknowledges existing institutions are inadequate for managing AI's cross-sectional nature affecting tax, labor, health, national security, and education
- Gates says he would support 'a credible plan for slowing down AI advances globally' if one existed
- After staying relatively quiet on AI issues recently, Gates vows to use his influence to get AI and equity higher on the public agenda
- He plans to raise AI issues with lawmakers during visits to Washington, DC and meetings with world leaders
- Gates admits the world is 'not remotely ready' for what's coming with AI and 'we are not preparing for it'
