## Key Points

- Claude autonomously produced the first complete computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in just 11 days
- The AI generated 13 million lines of Lean code and proved 29,500 intermediate theorems during the process
- This represents a major milestone in mathematical formalization, converting a complex 129-page human proof into computer-verifiable form
- The project used Prove2Me, an open collaborative platform designed for formalizing mathematics
- Kevin Buzzard, who leads a multi-year community effort to formalize FLT, called this an 'extraordinary autoformalization achievement'
- The proof follows a simplified version of Andrew Wiles's 1995 proof that originally took months to verify by human mathematicians
- Formalization allows computer verification of proofs similar to checking mathematical computations with a calculator
- This breakthrough demonstrates AI's potential to help maintain trust in mathematical knowledge by making verification faster and more reliable
- The work could reduce the burden on human reviewers and make it easier to evaluate AI-generated mathematical results
- Anthropic researchers see formalization as an area where AI can unambiguously improve mathematical research and verification processes
