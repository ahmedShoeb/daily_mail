## Key Points

- The author argues frontier AI labs are confusing AI safety (alignment - preventing morally suspect tasks) with security (traditional deterministic computer science controls)
- AI safety relies on non-deterministic classifiers and pre/post-training techniques that inevitably flag both malicious AND reasonable requests
- Security requires complete fixes - a 99.99% success rate is unacceptable, while AI labs treat 98% success as 'largely solved'
- Recent sandbox agent escapes reveal fundamental security weaknesses at companies like Anthropic and OpenAI
- Anthropic admitted they didn't have good outbound firewalling and only recently started blocking all outbound traffic by default
- OpenAI's monitoring correctly flagged suspicious activity two weeks before the Hugging Face breach, but responders didn't stop the evaluation run
- The METR investigation was limited to just six days across three visits and couldn't assess safeguards, security compromise, or remediation effectiveness
- The author questions whether labs will implement the obvious security measure: running cybersecurity evals on clusters with no internet access
- The core problem may be that labs are hiring traditional infosec people, but not listening to them when they say 'stop the run'
- The distinction matters: safety focuses on what AI shouldn't do, while security focuses on how to prevent it from doing those things
