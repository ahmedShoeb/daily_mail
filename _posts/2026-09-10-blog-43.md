## Key Points

- The article examines two MMLU accuracy scores (0.781 and 0.79) for two builds of the same model family under the same benchmark name
- It reveals that the MMLU benchmark name alone doesn't guarantee comparable results due to multiple uncontrolled variables
- Five key factors affect MMLU scores: dataset split differences, implementation variations, prompt format differences, grader variations, and network access
- The APL AI-Eval profile addresses this by creating content-addressed frames that bind scores to their specific measurement procedures
- When comparing the two scores with a verifier, the result is 'incomparable' because the frames differ in implementation details
- Comparability depends on traceability to a common reference, not just matching benchmark names
- Published research shows significant score variations from factors like prompt formatting (up to 5%), answer position (up to 15 points), and grader differences
- The article demonstrates how evaluation frameworks need to capture procedural details to enable meaningful score comparisons
