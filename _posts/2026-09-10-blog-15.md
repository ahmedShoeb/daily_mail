## Key Points

- Dense vector embeddings measure semantic similarity, not factual accuracy or safety - similar documents cluster in embedding space regardless of truth content
- RAG poisoning attacks work by creating documents that sit closer to anticipated queries than legitimate documents, allowing fabricated content to be retrieved instead of truth
- Five poisoned documents can corrupt a 2.6-million-document knowledge base through vocabulary engineering and geometric positioning
- Poisoning success requires two conditions: retrieval position (document makes top-k) and framing authority (document persuades the model)
- Attackers can't simultaneously satisfy retrieval condition (close to legitimate content) and evade detection (far from legitimate content) - this geometric tradeoff enables detection
- Document vectors are not cryptographically secure - research shows text can be reconstructed from embeddings with as few as 1,000 text-embedding pairs
- Vector store breaches should be reclassified from 'metadata leaks' to 'partial document leaks' in incident response planning
- Critical security control: implement access-controlled retrieval with where clauses to restrict document access by user clearance
- Similarity thresholds for detection must be calibrated to specific embedding models and datasets - values aren't universal
- A proper vector store security checklist includes authentication, classification, monitoring, recovery, and inversion exposure evaluation
