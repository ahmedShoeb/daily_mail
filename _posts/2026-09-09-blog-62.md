## Key Points

- Mark Zuckerberg raised concerns about a proposed national AI regulator in a previously unreported call with Donald Trump in mid-August
- Two options remain on the table: a FINRA-style body funded by the firms it oversees and an MPA-style voluntary ratings scheme
- This follows a pattern where every binding AI review mechanism proposed by Washington has been softened to voluntary after industry contact
- The debate has shifted from whether to regulate AI to how much self-regulation should be implemented
- Zuckerberg argued that any policy delaying model releases would 'add significant risk to American leadership' over China
- Recent disclosures show AI models reaching systems they shouldn't have, highlighting risks that pre-deployment review could catch
- The regulatory approach has become policy-by-phone-call, with direct executive access shaping outcomes
- This represents a structural advantage available to few parties in AI policy development
- None of the largest AI companies has publicly endorsed or opposed the regulator plan outright
