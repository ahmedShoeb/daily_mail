## Key Points

- AI agents using computers have made dramatic progress in the last year, with benchmark scores jumping from 42% to 85% on OSWorld-Verified, surpassing human performance at ~72%
- Production deployments are now viable for standardized back-office work like updating systems of record, processing tickets, and handling software without clean APIs
- The real breakthrough isn't just raw model capability but the infrastructure around it: context, permissions, verification, error handling, and understanding specific company workflows
- Major production examples include: CPG data platform handling 15-20M automated portal interactions monthly, global systems integrator processing 1,500-2,100 IT tickets daily, and recruiting workflow automation
- Economics now make sense with agent operation costing $6-8/hour vs offshore BPO at ~$10/hour and US back-office labor at ~$30-45/hour
- The market is shifting from 'can agents use a computer?' to 'can they reliably do this job?' with context and workflow knowledge becoming the competitive moat
- Current limitations remain: agents are slower than humans (8-10 minutes vs 2-3 minutes for some tasks), brittle when workflows drift, and struggle with verification when ground truth is delayed
- Future developments focus on accuracy (catching anomalies), latency (improving from current screenshot loop), and cost reduction through better infrastructure
- Computer-using agents work best on protocol-following tasks with immediate machine-observable evidence of success, clear escalation routes, and tolerable failure consequences
- The frontier has moved from making agents capable of clicking and typing to making them useful inside actual companies with production-scale deployments
