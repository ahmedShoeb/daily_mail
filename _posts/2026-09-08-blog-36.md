## Key Points

- ByteDance is developing an AI model for real-time spatial video generation that could launch as soon as next month, with founder Zhang Yiming personally overseeing the project
- The model would generate interactive virtual worlds for live streams, short-form dramas, and games, targeting 20 frames per second with 0.05 second latency
- Content will be rendered in the cloud rather than on devices, lowering hardware costs and computational load on headsets
- ByteDance owns Pico (its XR arm), and the model is meant to generate worlds responsive to Pico users' voices and movements
- World models were identified as ByteDance's top AI priority for 2026, with the company reportedly spending three to four times more than rivals in this area
- The company is pursuing two approaches: vision-language-action for robotics/embodied intelligence, and 3D simulation for entertainment/games
- ByteDance recently secured a $30 billion loan and is considering up to $70 billion in AI capital expenditure
- This approach shifts competition from hardware specs to models, cloud capacity, and content distribution - areas where ByteDance already has strengths
- The strategy potentially challenges Meta and Apple's high-end headset approaches by making fidelity a cloud problem rather than device problem
- ByteDance aims to ship at least one world model by year-end and measure it against Google's Genie
