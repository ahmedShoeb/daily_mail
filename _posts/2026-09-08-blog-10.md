## Key Points

- Claude AI completed the first complete computer-checked proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in just 11 days, working largely autonomously
- The formalization involved converting Andrew Wiles' 129-page 1995 proof into computer-readable Lean code that machines can verify
- Claude produced 13 million lines of Lean code and proved 29,500 intermediate theorems to complete the end-to-end proof
- The project demonstrates AI's potential to formalize complex mathematical proofs that would take human mathematicians years
- This achievement marks significant progress toward automating the verification of mathematical results and reducing review burdens
- Claude followed a simplified version of Wiles' proof from Darmon, Diamond, and Taylor with minimal human guidance
- The proof was verified using Lean's three standard axioms and matches Mathlib's statement of Fermat's Last Theorem
- Previous human efforts to formalize Fermat's Last Theorem were expected to take years, with a community project started in 2024
- The formalization could help catch errors in existing mathematical literature and verify AI-generated mathematical results
- This work represents the largest Lean proof ever constructed and suggests collaborative formalization of major results is now achievable
