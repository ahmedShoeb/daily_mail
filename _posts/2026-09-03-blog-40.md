## Key Points

- Major music publishers including Sony, EMI, and Warner Chappell are suing Anthropic, alleging the AI company got off too lightly with a $1.5 billion settlement for pirating content
- Publishers argue that $1.5 billion isn't enough to deter infringement for a company with a $2 trillion valuation
- Anthropic allegedly pirated 'thousands upon thousands' of copyrighted musical compositions alongside the 7 million books they previously admitted to
- The company allegedly plans to keep pirated content 'forever' to train its AI models, including songbooks and sheet music
- Music publishers claim songwriters are harmed by competing with AI-generated works in music charts
- Specific pirated content mentioned includes complete works of the Beatles, Taylor Swift's best songs, and VH1's 100 Greatest Songs of Rock & Roll
- Anthropic co-founder Benjamin Mann allegedly personally used BitTorrent to download millions of pirated books from Library Genesis (LibGen) starting in July 2021
- CEO Dario Amodei allegedly approved the torrenting, and both are named individually as defendants in the lawsuit
- The lawsuit cites internal Anthropic staff chats that apparently extol piracy, with references to Zlibrary as 'my beloved'
- Music publishers seek an injunction to stop Anthropic from continuing to train its AI on pirated musical content
