## Key Points

- Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) represent substantial innovation and can run directly on robots without network dependencies, but face a structural data problem
- Unlike language models trained on existing text, every robotic manipulation training example must be physically produced - one episode at a time at physical reality speeds
- Research shows robot success rates that are around 95% collapse to below 30% or even zero with modest scene perturbations, revealing memorization rather than generalization
- Traditional industrial robots work extremely reliably but achieve this by not generalizing - they're programmed for specific fixtures and environments
- Current humanoid robot deployments like BMW's Figure 02 units show limited operational hours (about 4 hours/day over 11 months), indicating they're still experimental
- The key challenge isn't making robots work, but making general-purpose robots work reliably across varied conditions
- Startups should focus on narrow, bounded applications rather than general-purpose robots to manage data collection needs and tail risks
- Large industrial robot installations (4.66 million worldwide) represent untapped training data potential if properly instrumented
- Physical AI requires much higher reliability standards (well above 99.9%) than language models due to safety concerns and financial risks
- The timeline for truly general-purpose robots appears long, with narrow deployments likely to succeed first before broader generalization
