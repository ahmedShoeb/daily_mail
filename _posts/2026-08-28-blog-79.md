## Key Points

- OpenAI and Anthropic are expected to control most of the world's usable FLOPs (compute power) within the next few years, currently taking about 30% of incremental compute and projected to reach 40-50% by next year.
- The labs have dramatically improved their economics, moving from venture-funded losses to profitability, with revenue per megawatt increasing from negative margins to $50M+ for Anthropic.
- Compute prices are expected to rise as labs can outbid others due to superior monetization capabilities, potentially reaching $25-$50M per megawatt compared to current $10-$15M.
- There's a significant shift from inference compute to R&D/training compute as labs reinvest profits into building AGI rather than maximizing immediate returns.
- China currently gets less than 10% of new compute due to export controls but may see a hockey stick growth in compute production by 2028-2029, though their chips are less advanced.
- The massive AI infrastructure buildout ($11T+ CapEx through 2029) could cause a sovereign debt crisis as hyperscalers compete for capital, potentially raising interest rates and crashing non-AI equities.
- The effective AI population (in terms of labor equivalence) at frontier labs is increasing 10x year over year, potentially leading to massive concentration of "intellectual labor" in just 2-3 companies.
- Centralization forces are powerful due to economies of scale in training, compute scarcity, and eventually continual learning and recursive self-improvement (RSI).
- Regulation and government intervention could slow AI progress, particularly through restrictions on model releases and data center approvals.
- The discussion explores whether AI will lead to extreme centralization of power or if decentralized alternatives can counter these centralizing forces.
