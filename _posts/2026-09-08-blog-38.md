## Key Points

- The paper argues that current natural language interfaces to databases are optimized for the wrong problem
- Traditional Text-to-SQL systems assume users ask questions answerable by single SQL queries
- In practice, users need help solving data problems through sequences of queries with intermediate reasoning
- The authors introduce a new paradigm called 'data investigations' for NL interfaces to data
- They present D^2, the first prototype system that autonomously searches, reasons over, and collects data
- The system uses a benchmark based on the Murder Mystery dataset to demonstrate capabilities
- D^2 handles evidence-backed decision making tasks beyond traditional single-query question answering
- This approach represents a fundamental shift from query execution to problem-solving assistance
