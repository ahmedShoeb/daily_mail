## Key Points

- Anthropic is preparing to give CEO Dario Amodei and other co-founders a class of stock with extra voting power to insulate them from external shareholder pressure
- The company will maintain its existing body of non-shareholder trustees who can elect a majority of board members
- This would be the first time Anthropic's leaders would have extra voting power
- Anthropic's co-founders hold relatively small ownership compared to other tech founders, with Amodei owning only about 2% of the company
- Dual-class structures giving supervoting power to leaders are common at founder-led companies like SpaceX and Meta
- The move comes as Anthropic prepares for a potential IPO later this year, expected to be one of the biggest market debuts in history
- Anthropic is structured as a public benefit corporation, legally required to balance commercial success with social benefit
- The company has a Long-Term Benefit Trust to ensure it delivers on its public benefit mission
- The specific voting arrangements are still being finalized and could change
- This structure would give founders greater control and protect them from short-term shareholder pressure
