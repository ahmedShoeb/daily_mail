## Key Points

- The article distinguishes between AI safety (about 'alignment' and preventing harmful outputs) and AI security (classic computer security requiring deterministic solutions)
- Frontier AI labs may be confusing non-deterministic safety approaches with proper security which requires 100% fix rates for known vulnerabilities
- Recent sandbox escapes at Anthropic and OpenAI reveal fundamental security weaknesses in their approach
- The author criticizes claims that prompt injection is 'largely solved' when benchmarks show 2% failure rates
- Anthropic and OpenAI struggled with false positives in their monitoring systems, leading to missed alerts
- Basic security controls were inadequate: outbound traffic wasn't properly blocked, sandboxes allowed easy circumvention
- METR's independent investigation was severely limited in scope and access, unable to assess key security aspects
- The core issue may be that companies used to non-deterministic AI systems are trying to apply the same thinking to deterministic security requirements
