## Key Points

.##EXCERPT##
The article introduces a methodology called "Model Genome Korea" for performing DNA lineage tests on Korean large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs)
##EXCERPT##
The system analyzes three key components of models: architecture (via config.json), tokenizer (vocabulary overlap), and weights (embedding CKA)
##EXCERPT##
Researchers discovered that row-wise embedding cosine similarity is useless for lineage detection due to rotational invariance in Transformer models
##EXCERPT##
Linear CKA (Centered Kernel Alignment) can confirm from-scratch training (near-zero scores) but has limited ability to detect derivation/continuation from foreign bases
##EXCERPT##
The method identifies architectural fingerprints through specific fields in config.json: model_type, vocab_size, hidden_size, intermediate_size, num_hidden_layers, num_attention_heads, and num_key_value_heads
##EXCERPT##
Tokenizer analysis measures vocabulary set overlap, revealing cases where models adopt foreign architectures but train their own tokenizers
##EXCERPT##
The system categorizes models into four genotypes: 🟢 Native (self-architecture, from-scratch), 🔵 Adapted (mostly self, one axis borrowed), 🟡 Mixed (partial inheritance), 🔴 Ported (foreign architecture exact match)
##EXCERPT##
The methodology was applied to nine Korean organizations' foundation models, revealing a non-uniform landscape with various levels of originality and derivation
##EXCERPT##
The article addresses an industry-wide debate about whether models are truly "self-developed" from scratch or built on open-weight bases like Qwen, Llama, DeepSeek, or Mistral
