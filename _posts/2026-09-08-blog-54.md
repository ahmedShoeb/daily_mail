## Key Points

- Google's TPUv7 Ironwood delivers up to 50% better performance per dollar compared to NVIDIA B200/B300 GPUs in apples-to-apples inference comparisons
- Ironwood is the first TPU generation where Google actively competes for external inference workloads, allowing customers to purchase or rent TPUs through GCP
- Anthropic has committed to over one million TPUs (400k+ purchased, 600k+ rented), making them the biggest TPU user and surpassing DeepMind by 2029
- The new TorchTPU stack represents a major improvement over previous TPU software, enabling native PyTorch support rather than requiring translation through JAX
- At 100 tokens/s/user interactivity, Ironwood costs $0.181 per million tokens vs $0.222 for B200 and $0.276 for B300
- TPUv7 lacks native FP4 support, giving NVIDIA an advantage on FP4 workloads, but TPUv8i will add FP4 support to compete with Rubin NVL72
- The TPU architecture features a 3D torus network topology that enables direct chip-to-chip communication across entire pods
- Major optimizations include DP attention improvements, MoE routing enhancements, Gated DeltaNet kernel optimizations, and better memory management
- Google is working on externalizing key features like speculative decoding (MTP) and prefill-decode disaggregation with KV-cache offloading
- The external TPU software stack is expected to be open-sourced around mid-October 2026 during the PyTorch Conference
