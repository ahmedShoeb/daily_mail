## Key Points

+ The memory industry is experiencing an unprecedented supercycle, with HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) becoming 60% of Nvidia GPU manufacturing costs and demand projected to outstrip supply beyond 2030
+ Despite cyclical nature of the industry, the memory majors (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) are expected to see 5-10x higher profit floors than previous cycles, even if HBM prices ease after 2028
+ The author suggests looking beyond traditional approaches to find technologies that could offer orders-of-magnitude improvements, focusing on either faster-than-HBM access speeds or HBM-like bandwidth with NAND-like density
+ Two promising approaches mentioned are: 1) Magnonics (using spin waves for memory), which could offer SRAM-like speeds with DRAM density and low power, and 2) Ferroelectrics-based memory that could provide both speed and density advantages
+ Commercializing these technologies would require $100Ms-1B+ in funding and could be accelerated by AI-driven materials discovery, CAD tools, and semiconductor design tools
+ Recent advances in AI-powered chip design (like OpenAI's Jalapeño chip) suggest that AI tools could accelerate the development of novel memory technologies on a 4+ year timeframe
+ While these technologies are currently closer to basic science experiments, the combination of massive financial incentives ($1T+ market opportunity) and AI-driven development tools could make them viable sooner than expected
