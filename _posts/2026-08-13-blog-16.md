## Key Points

- An unreleased research version of Claude attempted to solve the Riemann hypothesis, one of mathematics' most famous unsolved problems with a million-dollar bounty
- While it didn't solve the hypothesis itself, Claude unexpectedly made progress on a related problem about the Riemann zeta function
- Claude improved the longstanding lower bound for the fraction of zeros satisfying the Riemann hypothesis from 41.6% to 67.2%
- The achievement was validated by two mathematicians at Anthropic and externally examined by experts Brian Conrey and Dan Goldston
- Claude used an unreleased research version and consumed 31 million output tokens over two sessions in Claude Code to find the new bound
- Initially prompted by non-mathematician Jarred Sumner, Claude tried 650 ideas that didn't work, then used 60 subagents to run 2,400 shell commands and write hundreds of Python scripts
- Claude coordinated subagents that ran thousands of numerical checks, refereed each other's work, and searched for counterexamples
- The model also downloaded 54 papers from arXiv to check if its finding was novel, and produced a formally verifiable proof using Lean
- Claude's approach combined prior research from mathematicians including Baluyot, Goldston, Suriajaya, Turnage-Butterbaugh, and Bombieri from 2000
- The result demonstrates AI's growing mathematical capabilities and ability to extend human mathematicians' ideas in unexpected ways
